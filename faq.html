Free Snake Relocation FAQ

This Free Snake Relocation FAQ explains how to find help, contact an independent volunteer, update a directory listing, and make safer decisions around snakes.

This is a public directory, not a dispatch service.

Free Snake Relocation does not assign, dispatch, employ, supervise, train, screen, certify, insure, or guarantee any listed person. You choose whom to contact, and all communication and assistance are directly between you and that independent volunteer.

1 About the Directory

What is the Free Snake Relocation Directory?

It is a public informational directory that connects people who need snake removal help with independent volunteers who have chosen to publish their contact information. The website provides the connection only. It does not arrange or manage the relocation.

Will the directory dispatch someone to me?

No. Use the Find Help page, select your state and city, and contact a listed volunteer directly. The administrators do not choose a volunteer, send anyone to your property, schedule visits, or monitor the response.

Does an approved listing mean the person was vetted or endorsed?

No. Administrators review submissions before publication, but approval means only that the listing was accepted for the directory. It is not a background check, identity verification, endorsement, certification, or guarantee of skill, insurance, conduct, or safety.

How do I find a volunteer near me?

Open Find Help and choose your state and city. You can also browse the volunteer map on that page. If your city has no listing, check nearby cities because some volunteers may travel beyond the city shown.

Is a volunteer guaranteed to be available?

No. Volunteers manage their own schedules, and the directory does not show live availability. A listing does not guarantee a response or a particular response time. If one person does not answer, try another nearby listing.

Is the relocation really free?

The directory is for volunteer snake relocation without a required fee. Free Snake Relocation does not collect payments or set prices. Any optional tip or gift is a private choice between you and the volunteer, not a condition of receiving help. You can report a listing concern if someone requires payment while using this directory.

What areas does the directory cover?

The directory includes volunteers in many parts of the United States, but coverage varies by location and changes as listings are added, updated, or removed. Search the current directory rather than relying on an older screenshot or map pin.

2 Joining and Updating a Listing

How do I join the volunteer directory?

Open the Join or Update page, read the volunteer requirements and disclaimer, and submit the application. Your submission goes to an administrator for review before anything is published.

What do I do if I move or change my phone number?

Submit the same volunteer form again, but choose “I'm already listed, but need to update my information.” Enter your current information in full. After an administrator reviews and approves the update, the existing listing is updated rather than creating a second active listing.

Should I register as a new volunteer again when my information changes?

No. Choose the update option on the form. This gives administrators a written record of the change and helps prevent duplicate listings.

Can an administrator just edit my listing for me?

Administrators may correct an obvious formatting mistake, but moves, phone changes, and other meaningful updates should normally be submitted through the form. The form creates a trackable record showing what the volunteer requested.

How do I ask to be removed from the directory?

Use the Contact page and include the name, phone number, city, and state shown on the listing. Administrators need enough information to identify the correct record before removing it.

How long will approval or an update take?

Submissions require administrator review, so there is no guaranteed publication time. The searchable directory may reflect an approved change before the separate Google map finishes updating.

3 Snake Safety and Identification

What should I do if I see a snake?

Stay calm, keep people and pets away, and give the snake space. If you can do so without approaching it, take a clear photo using your camera zoom and watch from a safe location. Do not touch, corner, trap, or try to handle an unidentified snake.

Are all snakes dangerous?

No. Many snakes are nonvenomous and play an important role in their ecosystems. However, identification can be difficult, and any snake may bite when threatened. Treat an unidentified snake cautiously and do not handle it.

Can the website identify a snake from a photo?

The website provides educational resources, but it does not guarantee an identification. You can review the Snake ID page and share a safely taken photo with a local expert or the volunteer you contact. Never move closer just to get a better picture.

Can I kill a snake if I feel threatened?

Keep your distance and move people and pets away instead of approaching the snake. Wildlife laws vary by state, species, and situation, and killing or possessing a native snake may be restricted. Review the state law resources and contact the appropriate wildlife authority for legal guidance.

What is a safe distance to relocate a snake?

There is no single distance that is appropriate for every snake, species, habitat, season, or jurisdiction. Research has found that translocation outcomes vary and can be affected by homing behavior, unfamiliar habitat, movement after release, and the suitability of the release site.

The person performing a relocation is responsible for following applicable law and using species-appropriate practices. Free Snake Relocation does not set relocation distances or protocols.

Research background: Germano and Bishop's review of amphibian and reptile translocations and Harvey and colleagues' snake relocation study.

What should I do after a snakebite?

Treat a snakebite as a medical emergency. Call 911 or seek emergency medical attention immediately, and do not drive yourself. Do not try to catch or kill the snake. If possible, take a photo from a safe distance.

Do not apply ice or a tourniquet, cut the wound, or try to suck out venom. Follow instructions from emergency professionals. See the CDC snakebite guidance.

Immediate danger or a snakebite?

Do not wait for a directory volunteer. Call 911 or the appropriate emergency service. For urgent animal control concerns, contact local animal control, a wildlife authority, or a licensed provider as appropriate.

4 Contacting a Volunteer

How do I request help?

Search by state and city on the Find Help page. Choose a listing and text that volunteer directly. Free Snake Relocation does not receive the request, match you with someone, or contact volunteers on your behalf.

Why is texting preferred instead of calling?

Many volunteers do not answer calls from unknown numbers. A text also creates a written record of the conversation, including the location, photos, timing, and what each person agreed to. The website and its administrators cannot see or store those private messages.

What should I include in my first text?

Include your city or neighborhood, where the snake is located, when it was last seen, whether people or pets are nearby, and whether you have a photo taken from a safe distance. Share the exact address after the volunteer confirms they may be able to help.

Copy this example: Hi, I found your listing on FreeSnakeRelocation.com. I am in [city or neighborhood]. There is a snake [where it is located], last seen [time]. I can send a photo. Are you available to help?
What if no one responds?

Try another listing in the same area or check a nearby city. Volunteers may be working, sleeping, driving, outside their service area, or otherwise unavailable. You can also contact local animal control, a wildlife agency, or a licensed pest control provider. If anyone is in immediate danger, call emergency services.

Can the directory settle a disagreement with a volunteer?

No. Communication and assistance are directly between you and the independent volunteer. Administrators do not supervise the interaction or decide private disputes. You may still use the Contact page to report a serious concern about a public listing.

Is Free Snake Relocation responsible for a volunteer's actions?

No. Listed volunteers act independently and are not employees, agents, contractors, or representatives of Free Snake Relocation. Read the full directory disclaimer before using a listing.
